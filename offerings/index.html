What we do

We help our clients own the tools they depend on. There are three ways to work with us, and most projects use more than one.

Advise

For organizations deciding what to build, adopt or leave alone. We look at what you need, what it will cost to keep running, and what it means for your privacy and your people.

This can include

  • Technology decisions. Whether to build, buy, adopt or walk away, and what each choice commits you to.
  • Privacy and security. Where your data goes, who can reach it, and what to change first.
  • Research and evidence review. Close reading of papers, sources and claims, with every judgement traceable.
  • Ethics and governance. How a group decides, who owns what, and how value is counted.

You leave with

Our advice in writing, with the sources behind each part of it.

Shown in

  • Vetiver Examines a medical paper for validity and bias, and shows where every judgement came from
  • The Aion Apparatus An instrument for examining the ethics of made things
  • Ethical Imagination Trust, authorship and ethics, written into code
  • research-manager A Mac app for deciding what each research thread needs next

Build

We get a working version in front of the people who will use it in days, not months. Then we build the real thing so your team can run it without us.

This can include

  • Prototypes. Something real to try with your people before anyone commits to the full build.
  • AI agent workflows. Agents that do a defined job inside your work, on models you can run yourselves.
  • Local AI. Models that run on your own machines, so your data stays with you.
  • Archives and knowledge systems. A collection or an organization's memory, organized so it can be found and kept.
  • Community infrastructure. Servers, networks and local-first software a group runs itself.

You leave with

Running software, its source code, and the notes whoever maintains it will need.

Shown in

  • daimon A coding agent that runs entirely on your own machine
  • Earshot A speech studio that runs on your own machine
  • caer-stack Infrastructure a community runs for itself
  • Corpusmith Files a folder tree through a plan you review, and can move it all back
  • Meridian A personal knowledge OS
  • Haunted Geography Memorial plaques you meet by standing where it happened

Teach

Workshops and courses for teams who want to build and look after their own tools, including how to work well with AI coding assistants.

This can include

  • Courses and syllabi. Written for the people who will take them, at their pace.
  • Workshops. Sessions where a group learns a practice by doing it together.

You leave with

Course materials you can teach again without us.

Shown in

  • Training A beginner's course in building small apps by describing them to a coding assistant
