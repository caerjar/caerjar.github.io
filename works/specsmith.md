Specsmith assembles a specification from transcripts, design material, existing code, and recorded decisions. Each retained claim includes a quotation and source location that the software checks against the supplied material.

## The problem

A product's requirements rarely live in one document. They arrive through conversations, screens, spreadsheets, existing implementations, and changes of mind. A useful specification needs to retain where a statement came from and make disagreements visible.

## The approach

Specsmith produces a specification pack whose claims can be traced back to their sources. A quotation that cannot be located is reported and excluded from the retained claim set. Recorded decisions and source precedence help organize changes across the material.

The demonstration uses Tidepool, an invented client with its own calls, design material, code, and scope changes. This makes the workflow inspectable without exposing confidential client information.

## What the example establishes

The workflow demonstrates source tracking, quotation checks, and reproducible document assembly. These checks establish where cited language occurs. Human review remains responsible for deciding whether a quotation supports the interpretation and whether the specification answers the actual need.

## Outputs

A specification, feature matrix, wireframes, and HTML and PDF documentation, with source references available for review.
